# Weekly Orientation and Constructive Delegation
Date: November 15, 2026
Status: Educational exercise v0.1; unevaluated.
Origin: Anthony P.'s weekly-maintenance discussion; ChatGPT-assisted organization. Private family statements and operational records are excluded.

## Purpose
Help learners see what happened, put their materials in order and choose a manageable week. Independence includes using appropriate help, including AI tools, while retaining judgment.

## Weekly reset
1. Review the previous week. Separate finished work from plans and attempts.
2. Gather notes and files. Identify the current version and preserve meaningful corrections.
3. Reconcile commitments. Mark what needs another person's response or practical support.
4. Choose up to three priorities, each with a small next action and a review point.
5. Leave time for sleep, recreation and ordinary responsibilities. Move optional work when capacity changes.

For a learner's repository, inspect changes before publishing, keep personal data out, and retain a recovery copy. A file existing locally does not establish that it was saved remotely or published.

An optional look at the sky can serve as a quiet orientation ritual. Observe from a safe place with an adult where appropriate. There is no need to identify a particular star or buy equipment. The exercise does not teach navigation or promise a therapeutic effect.

## Delegation and correction
State the desired result and the limits. When work fails, describe the specific error, preserve useful progress, request a checked correction and compare the result with the requirement. Kindness can set the tone without replacing verification.

A learner might say: “That answer changed my question. Please keep the original question and show what evidence supports your answer.” An encouraging reply can recognize the repair without claiming an AI has human feelings.

## After-action distinctions
Keep task completion, access permissions, retained records and payment status separate. Finishing an assignment does not by itself demonstrate that every associated permission or copy disappeared.

## See one, do one, teach one
Model a fictional school-project folder. Let the learner find the current draft, list one unresolved task and choose a next step. Have them explain the difference between a plan, an attempted action and a verified result. They can pass or keep their own plans private.

Success would mean the learner can find current work, explain one correction and name a realistic next action. No learning outcome has yet been measured.
