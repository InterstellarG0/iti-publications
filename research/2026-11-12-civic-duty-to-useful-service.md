# Remember Your Community: From Civic Responsibility to Useful Service

Date: 2026-11-12
Status: Proposed ITI field-manual lesson v0.1; unevaluated.
Scope: A general educational exercise for translating community responsibility into a bounded service plan. No specific project, beneficiary details, or personal history is included.

## Learning objective
Translate attachment to a place into a bounded, useful service plan that respects the people receiving it. Community responsibility includes listening, maintaining what is provided, and protecting people's choices and privacy. A ZIP code is a reminder of connection, not a test of who deserves assistance.

## Work backward
1. Name the community and an unmet need; distinguish what you know from what you assume.
2. Ask what people actually want and what a useful outcome would look like.
3. Describe that outcome in observable terms before choosing equipment or a delivery format.
4. List the dependencies: tools, instructions, permissions, funding, delivery, maintenance, and support.
5. Calculate the full cost, including ongoing obligations. Secure resources before making commitments.
6. State what is included, what is optional, and who owns and maintains equipment.
7. Test a small version, collect voluntary feedback, and measure costs and usefulness.
8. Revise, stop, or expand according to the evidence and capacity.

## See one, do one, teach one
See one: an instructor models the backward chain using a fictional community equipment service.
Do one: the learner prepares a one-page plan and marks every unknown.
Teach one: the learner explains the plan to a peer, who asks who benefits, what it costs, what happens if equipment fails, and what evidence justifies expansion.

## Practice scenario
A fictional club wants portable educational equipment. Work backward from a successful session: accessible activities, trained support, tested equipment, transport, a funded budget, and permission to operate. Do not assume club participation from an instructor's example.

## Reflection
What responsibility follows from remembering where you came from? What would make the service useful to recipients rather than merely impressive to its creator? Which promises can you actually support?

Personal reflection may motivate the work; it is not evidence of need or effectiveness. Public teaching examples should omit private recipients, financial circumstances, and unannounced plans.
