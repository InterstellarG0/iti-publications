# A Small Satellite Starts With a Mission Question
Date: November 15, 2026
Status: Learning question v0.1; no funded mission, procurement or engineering result.
Origin: Founder interest in smaller satellite experiments; AI-assisted synthesis.

Could a bounded small-satellite experiment answer a useful question for a future educational network? Begin with the question and required evidence before selecting spacecraft hardware.

NASA's CubeSat Launch Initiative resource page provides introductory development material, including CubeSat 101. This is a learning reference, not evidence that ITI qualifies for a launch opportunity or has a feasible budget.

A useful study distinguishes mission payload from supporting systems and ground operations. A small experimental payload does not by itself establish broadband, gaming-service capacity or profitable cargo demand. Current mission costs, service permissions, launch options and environmental constraints require mission-specific verification.

A historical satellite article can motivate learning without supplying current procurement prices. No source illustrations or full article are redistributed here.

A no-spend first exercise is to write one hypothetical mission question, the data needed to answer it and the dependencies that could prevent a valid result. Keep portable-kit funding and testing as the near-term work.
