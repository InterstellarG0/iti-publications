# From Lived Experience to Research Questions: An Independent Author's Perspective

Date: November 13, 2026
Author: Anthony P., founder of Interstellar Technologies Initiative, LLC; ChatGPT-assisted drafting and source organization.
Status: Perspective and proposed publication method v0.1. Not a clinical study, case report, systematic review, treatment recommendation, or proof of efficacy.

## Author standpoint
My starting point is lived experience and the practical questions I bring from earlier work in volunteering, EMS, surgical-laser support, and pest control. These roles are self-reported background, not a claim of current licensure, physician qualification, or expertise in every medical discipline. I want those experiences to generate questions that others can examine and challenge.

First-person observations can make a problem visible. They cannot alone establish its prevalence, a diagnosis, the cause of a change, or whether an intervention works for others. Medical terminology helps formulate questions; knowing terminology does not settle them.

## A legitimate contribution with a defined scope
PCORI describes patients, caregivers, and community members with lived experience as partners in research, including people outside professional healthcare and research systems. This supports meaningful participation; it does not mean that any particular observation or product has been validated. [1]

The initial ITI contribution is therefore a labeled perspective: what I experienced, what remains uncertain, what question follows, and how someone might test it. Public release in this repository is self-publication, not journal acceptance or peer review.

## Proposed evidence ladder
1. **Perspective:** a self-described account, with exact scope and selected privacy boundaries.
2. **Structured self-observation:** a predefined log with dates, conditions, relevant changes, and uncertainties. Missing entries remain missing. Such a log does not by itself establish cause.
3. **Literature-informed hypothesis:** primary sources, alternative explanations, and a bounded prediction.
4. **Prototype feasibility:** measured usability and limitations, initially using synthetic material.
5. **Clinical research, if appropriate:** qualified collaboration, the relevant review and consent processes, and a suitable study design before making clinical claims.

None of these stages is automatically completed by publishing the previous one. Do not retroactively invent a dataset from recollection.

## Claim card for every manuscript
| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Statement | The exact observation or proposed claim |
| Evidence type | Recollection, contemporaneous record, external source, or measured result |
| Source and timing | Who reported it, when, and any transcript uncertainty |
| Other explanations | Plausible alternatives and contradictory observations |
| Scope | What the evidence supports and does not support |
| Privacy | What may be shared and what remains excluded |
| Next test | A bounded, appropriate evaluation |
| Review state | Self-published, externally reviewed, submitted, accepted, or peer-reviewed—only when true |

Example wording: “I recall choosing a creative activity while frustrated, and I perceived a change afterward.” That is different from claiming that the activity treats a disorder or that it caused the change.

## Publication and case-report boundaries
A personal essay is not automatically a medical case report. CARE guidance describes structured reporting and publication consent; its writing guidance also calls for de-identification. A future case report would need the appropriate records, consent, reporting detail, and target-journal requirements. This perspective does not claim CARE compliance. [2, 3]

Do not disclose former patients, coworkers, or identifiable incidents merely because they contributed to a question. Sensitive personal clinical details remain outside this release.
