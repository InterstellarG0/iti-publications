# Portable Experiences: Simulate, Rehearse, Book, Measure

Date: November 13, 2026
Status: Concept and field exercise v0.1; no working kit, paid pilot, partner, or measured result is asserted.
Origin: Founder-led scenario discussion; ChatGPT-assisted organization and critical review. This public adaptation excludes private transcripts and financial or medical history.

## Purpose
Explore a small portable astronomy experience with optional gaming, built around participant choice and a digital capture to take home. Physical printing and branded keepsakes are proposed additions subject to testing. The central question is whether people value and book a repeatable experience whose complete costs can be covered.

Simulation identifies questions; it does not establish demand, technical performance, safety, or profitability. A concept image helps explain an offer but must remain labeled as a concept.

## Sequence and readiness
1. Define the experience and a complete, modular equipment list.
2. Secure affordable funding and verify the shopping list before commitments.
3. Assemble and rehearse: unpack, power, select a suitable target, capture, save, optionally print, reset, and repack.
4. Confirm the actual session length, accessibility, supervision needs, transport fit, power and connectivity requirements, software permissions, and weather alternatives.
5. Approach suitable existing venues with tested equipment and an operating plan.
6. Offer a bounded pilot with confirmed bookings and measure actual results before expansion.

Venue-first means using an existing permitted site before investing in a dedicated facility. It does not move outreach ahead of equipment funding and testing.

## Proposed participant flow
A guest selects an experience emphasis, chooses from targets tested for the night, participates in framing or observes with guidance, and watches the capture develop. Gaming and questions are optional. A take-home image should identify the target, date, and capture context accurately. Do not present a stock image, earlier capture, or generated illustration as the guest's live capture.

Thirty minutes is a rehearsal target, not a guaranteed imaging turnaround. Distinguish elapsed session time, accepted exposure time, processing, printing, and reset. Do not promise detailed planetary or galaxy imagery until the selected equipment and conditions have demonstrated the deliverable. Weather may prevent capture; state rescheduling, refund, and alternative-experience choices before payment.

## One kit and one operator
Test sequential bookings with reset time. Group capacity is an open question; four attendees is not a proven capacity. An external monitor and two controllers do not establish simultaneous local multiplayer support. Test the actual games, devices, interfaces, and workload. An independent telescope-control path is a design goal requiring verification.

Maintain supervision of equipment and guests. A dark observing patch can coexist with a safe illuminated route, accessible seating, nearby staffed facilities, and clear property permission. A parking lot or park is not suitable merely because it is dark. Determine site permission and operating requirements before use.

## Booking and economics
Use a calendar the operator can manage directly, with a permanent booking link, one group per slot, buffers, booking cutoff, confirmations, and clear cancellation and weather terms. No provider or integration is selected here.

Choose and state the pricing unit: per person, pair, group, or host-funded session. Do not silently switch units when comparing prices.

For a planning period:
- Revenue is collected booking income, with taxes and refunds handled distinctly.
- Variable costs include consumables, processing charges, licenses where applicable, and agreed venue fees.
- Fixed session-night costs include travel and setup-related costs.
- Operator compensation includes travel, setup, hosting, reset, and breakdown.
- Equipment recovery and replacement reserves must be accounted for without counting them twice.

Test one-, two-, and three-booking scenarios. Gross receipts are not profit. Ownership removes an equipment rental payment; it does not remove wear, loss risk, maintenance, consumables, or the value of time. Recovering the equipment cost is not guaranteed.

Negotiate venue compensation around what is actually provided: space, power, facilities, promotion, or staffed custody. A proposed revenue share is not an industry benchmark. Define its base, duration, and exclusions; do not assume any owner has agreed.

## Access and audience
Use an interest question rather than a personality diagnosis or age stereotype. A couple may want a technical lesson; a solo guest may want gaming. Paid sessions can coexist with short discretionary demonstrations during unused capacity. Protect paid bookings and define supervision and scope for minors before youth activities.

## Custody and expansion
Initially attended service and later venue-operated rentals are distinct models. Before an unattended handoff, resolve custody, inventory, staff training, agreement, deposit/payment arrangements, and insurance suitability for the actual use and nonreturn risks. These are unresolved review items, not a statement that any policy pays a claim.

Test a second night or location before assuming another kit is needed. Replication requires reliable instructions, operator training, supported customer demand, and replacement capacity. If demand is weak, distinguish reach, booking friction, price, timing, venue, and product fit before changing everything at once. Set an affordable test limit and review point.

## Pilot evidence card
| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Offer and pricing unit | Exact version shown to guests |
| Inquiry source | Voluntary, minimal channel information |
| Stated interest | Separate from reservation and payment |
| Attendance and return interest | Counts, denominator, conditions |
| Timing | Setup, capture, service, reset, breakdown |
| Quality | Whether the promised takeaway was delivered |
| Costs and operator time | Actual amounts and unresolved items |
| Problems | Weather, access, controller, power, print, support |
| Decision | Continue, revise, defer, or stop, with a reason |

No customer database or pilot results accompany this note. A friendly expression of willingness to pay is feedback, not a sale or proof of market size.
