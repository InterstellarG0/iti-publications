# Drafts as Delegation: Capture, Place, Link, Return

**Author:** Anthony P., founder, Interstellar Technologies Initiative, LLC (ITI)  
**Date:** November 13, 2026  
**Status:** Voice Lab / Foundry design note v0.1; unevaluated, nonclinical  
**Preparation:** AI-assisted organization and drafting from a founder conversation; reviewed for attribution and evidence boundaries.

## The problem
Creating a document can feel like adding another unread task. A different use is to preserve context for a later human–AI handoff. The person need not read every page to ask a capable assistant to retrieve the source, summarize it, and identify a decision.

This is a design proposal, not a claim that every assistant can access every file. Retrieval depends on available tools, permissions, storage and the correct document version.

## A practical workflow
1. **Pause:** mark the current thread and where to resume.
2. **Capture:** record a new idea in the person's words; label assistant interpretations separately.
3. **Place:** give it a descriptive title, status and location.
4. **Link:** connect related ideas without asserting that association proves causation.
5. **Return:** reopen the saved source and summarize the chosen thread, unresolved questions and next decision.

A book rack is the metaphor: putting an idea on a shelf preserves it without requiring immediate action.

## Delegation card
> Capture this as a draft. Keep my account separate from your interpretation. When I return, find and open the current document, summarize what changed, and tell me what needs my decision.

A draft is not approval to buy, publish or implement its contents. Confirm successful saving, give the person a usable reference, and report missing or ambiguous retrieval honestly. Human corrections and rejected interpretations should remain traceable.

## Proposed interface
An idea card could show the original statement, interpretation, related cards, maturity, next question and a return-to-thread marker. Separate Draft, Decided, Tested and Released states. Make correction, export and deletion accessible. Avoid treating the number of documents or time spent talking as the success measure.

## Small nonclinical test
Use a fictional community event with several planning branches. Compare ordinary conversation with the proposed capture-and-return flow on similar tasks. Record whether the participant can locate the right draft, recover the main decision, correct a summary and resume the intended task. Track retrieval time, omitted constraints, factual errors, participant effort and mistaken assumptions of authorization. Also record whether organization itself adds burden.

No participants, controlled comparison, measurements or benefits are reported here. A founder's experience motivated the design; it is not a dataset or independent validation. The note makes no claim to diagnose or treat a condition. Any clinical intended use requires a separately defined and appropriately reviewed research proposal.
