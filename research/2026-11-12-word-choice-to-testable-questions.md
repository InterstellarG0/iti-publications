# From Word Choice to Testable Questions

Date: 2026-11-12
Status: Proposed research question and teaching exercise v0.1; unevaluated.
Scope: A general exercise in checking uncertain words, identifying assumptions, and formulating answerable questions. No personal exchange is reproduced.

## Research question
Can examining word choice help learners identify an argument's framing, surface assumptions, and formulate questions that evidence can address?

This is a proposed synthesis, not a claim that linguistic framing is a new field or that word choice reveals a speaker's hidden motives. A literature review is needed before asserting novelty or effectiveness.

## Field exercise
1. Preserve the exact statement and its source.
2. Confirm uncertain words with the speaker before interpreting them.
3. Identify which words describe observations and which introduce judgments or assumptions.
4. Offer more than one plausible interpretation.
5. Translate the claim into a bounded question with terms and evidence requirements.
6. Ask what evidence would change the answer.

Example: “This system is taking over” becomes “Which decisions can the system make, who authorized them, and how can permission be withdrawn?” This does not establish whether the original concern is correct.

## Evaluate the method
Compare original and revised questions for clarity, fidelity to the speaker, explicit assumptions, and answerability. Record mistakes and corrections. Do not treat confidence, eloquence, or agreement as accuracy.
