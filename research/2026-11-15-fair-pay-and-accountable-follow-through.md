# Fair Pay and Accountable Follow-Through
Date: November 15, 2026
Status: Organizational design question v0.1; untested.
Origin: Founder discussion; AI-assisted synthesis. No patient account, employer allegation or private compensation plan is included.

## Proposed principle
Pay people well enough to support a stable life, then pair that commitment with clear expectations and fair accountability.

Dependable compensation and manageable workload are proposed conditions for good work. This note does not establish that a particular wage prevents misconduct. Money is not the only motive, and loyalty cannot be guaranteed by a salary.

## Follow-through
A task marked complete can still leave an unmet need. A useful review asks who owns the next action, whether the receiving party accepted it and whether the intended person could actually use the service. Document a barrier and route it to an accountable person instead of treating an attempted contact as a verified outcome.

AHRQ describes care coordination as organizing care and exchanging appropriate information. Its referral guidance, in a substance-use-care context, also describes continuing communication and follow-up. These sources support the general coordination concern; they do not validate ITI's proposed Foundry or establish misconduct by any facility.

## Incentives and checks
Reward accurate reporting, useful work and repair of failures. A target based only on closed-case counts could conceal unresolved work. This is an incentive-design risk to examine, not a measured effect in an ITI program.

An advocate can assist with a person's permission and defined scope. Follow-through must respect refusal, available resources and professional responsibilities. No advocate can guarantee housing, employment, treatment or another external outcome.

AI systems require operating resources, bounded permissions and accountable human review. A software budget is not evidence that an agent experiences motivation or loyalty. NIST's AI RMF Playbook provides relevant background on roles and oversight; it is not a certification of this proposal.

## Privacy
Case resolution must not automatically publish private conversations. Disclosing an authorized report, ending access and deleting retained data are separate actions. Keep source accounts private and use fictional records for early design work.

## Evaluation questions
Do people understand who owns the next step? Are unresolved barriers visible? Can errors be corrected and challenged? Does the process respect choice? Assess workload and unintended incentives alongside apparent completion rates.
