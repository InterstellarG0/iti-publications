Source: live field conversation during third-party logistics work

## Field observation
An individual Roadie operator described coordinating eight physical items across a pickup location's account software and Roadie's software. The operator bridges systems physically; the conversation has not established whether independent confirmations or cryptographic signatures occurred.

## Hypotheses to test
1. A custody event can be independently attested without revealing unnecessary identity information.
2. Merchant, logistics platform, and recipient may each pay for distinct verification value; an illustrative $0.10 per party per item yields $0.30 gross, not validated revenue.
3. Revenue margin could subsidize discounts, network speed and privacy infrastructure, with baseline privacy guaranteed to all.
4. One human may operate in different legal capacities (individual contractor, authorized LLC representative) using separately scoped credentials and keys.
5. Software agents may perform delegated tasks across locations without implying literal remote viewing, impersonation, or unlimited authority.

## Architectural distinctions
- A three-sided fee model is not a three-signature cryptographic protocol.
- An email address does not establish contracting or tax capacity.
- EIN, LLC documentation, bank details and platform agreements can establish legal/payment authorization; they do not themselves change encryption.
- Keep human identity, legal entity, credential, agent delegation, and payment destination separate.
- Preserve auditability, revocation, consent, and legal accountability.
- 'Legend' here means a legitimate authorized operational role, not a fabricated identity.
