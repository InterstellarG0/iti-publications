# Preserve Context, Then Test the Design

Date: November 13, 2026
Status: Proposed documentation method and design exercise v0.1; unevaluated.
Origin: Founder-led discussion of complete accounts, interpretation, and teachable design; ChatGPT-assisted synthesis. This generalized release contains no personal case narrative or clinical dataset.

## Question
Can a documentation workflow preserve a speaker's intended meaning while producing bounded, correctable product hypotheses?

A brief quotation may omit later clarification. A long account may also contain uncertainty, contradiction, or error. Length alone establishes neither accuracy nor safety. The method therefore keeps source, interpretation, correction, and evidence distinct.

## Proposed record
| Layer | Contents | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Authorized original passage and context | Identify transcript gaps and transcription uncertainty |
| Speaker clarification | Intended meaning and corrections | Do not silently rewrite the original |
| Observation | What was reported or directly observed | Label self-report and distinguish it from independent evidence |
| Interpretation | One or more explanations | Attribute to the interpreter; preserve objections |
| Design hypothesis | Proposed feature and intended benefit | Do not convert an anecdote into established effectiveness |
| Evaluation | Test, conditions, outcome, limitations | Report neutral and adverse outcomes as well as successes |
| Publication | Specifically selected derivative | Preserve private source custody and permission boundaries |

A feeling, a thought, an intention, and an action should not be collapsed into one field. Nor should a broad statement erase specific evidence when there is a concrete concern. Clarification and accountable human judgment remain necessary.

## Fictional design exercise
A fictional user says they sometimes choose a creative building activity when frustrated. Possible interpretations include helpful pause, ordinary recreation, or avoidance of an unresolved task. A prototype might offer an optional creative activity and a user-chosen return point.

Test whether people understand the options, can stop easily, feel accurately represented, and return when they intended. Record when the feature is unhelpful or prolongs avoidance. This is a hypothesis about a reflection interface, not a diagnosis, treatment, or established account of emotional regulation.

## Privacy and user control
- Use synthetic examples for initial prototypes.
- Minimize raw sensitive data, define retention, and separate private source records from public derivatives.
- Let a person review interpretations and retain corrections alongside the source.
- Make optional sharing explicit and scoped; do not publish whole transcripts by default.
- A conversation summary is not a certified legal record, a clinical assessment, or evidence that an anticipated event will occur.
- Do not make claims about a person's future behavior from creative expression alone.
- If a future tool is intended for clinical settings, define that intended use and obtain appropriate independent clinical, privacy, and ethics review before participant research.

## Teaching sequence
See one: compare a source passage, an overconfident interpretation, and a corrected record.
Do one: label source, uncertainty, alternative explanations, and one bounded design question.
Teach one: explain what would count as evidence against the proposed benefit.

The objective is faithful, revisable documentation and testable design. It is not automatic diagnosis or conversion of every personal experience into a product.
