# Visualization as a Learning Tool: A Product Hypothesis

Date: November 13, 2026
Status: Nonclinical design and teaching proposal v0.1; no prototype evaluation, clinical effectiveness, customer demand, or revenue is established.
Origin: Founder-led design discussion; ChatGPT-assisted synthesis. Private experiences and clinical working drafts are excluded from this public adaptation.

## Proposed value
A scene-building interface might help people explain an imagined situation, label assumptions, compare interpretations, and practice a response. The initial proposal is a voluntary educational sandbox using fictional scenes. Its value would be clearer communication and reasoning if demonstrated in evaluation.

A scene is a representation. It does not verify an event, recover an accurate memory, diagnose a person, or establish the truth of a belief. AI-generated details must never be silently inserted as the participant's recollection.

## Example exercise
A fictional team is preparing a portable community event. One participant imagines an empty venue and concludes that the entire project will fail. Learners build the scene, label which details are known, and compare alternative explanations: poor awareness, unsuitable timing, booking friction, or an offer that does not fit the audience.

They choose one proportionate test and describe what result would change their interpretation. Neither the feared outcome nor the optimistic alternative is treated as established.

## Interface requirements
1. Separate the prompt, user-authored scene, AI suggestions, edits, interpretation, and independently checked information.
2. Label each element: imagined, reported, observed, inferred, contradicted, or unknown.
3. Offer alternative explanations without suggesting that agreement proves accuracy.
4. Let the participant correct, pause, leave, or decline optional sharing.
5. Avoid scoring private disclosures, rewarding escalating distress, or forcing reconstruction of sensitive experiences.
6. Use minimal retention and clear access boundaries. Offline operation is a proposed feature requiring technical verification, not an automatic privacy guarantee.
7. Keep fictional exercises distinct from real personal records.

## Privacy and resilience exercise
Ask: what information is needed, who will receive it, and what could happen if it is forwarded? Teach deliberate sharing without teaching that everyone is secretly recording the learner.

For youth-facing exercises, use fictional examples, age-appropriate supervision, and a trusted-adult route for concerns. A possible risk is not evidence of a particular intrusion. One confirmed concern supports that concern; it does not validate every other suspicion. The exercise should end with a practical decision, not repeated searching for threats.

## Evaluation before claims
| Proposed benefit | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|
| Clearer explanation | Participants and independent reviewers can distinguish fact, assumption, and fiction |
| Faithful representation | Participants can identify and correct misrepresentation |
| Better questions | Questions become specific and answerable without changing intended meaning |
| User control | Participants can pause, exit, and manage optional sharing as described |
| Practical usefulness | Participants and facilitators identify value beyond a simpler paper exercise |
| Sustainable delivery | Measured setup, facilitation, support, and maintenance costs fit a tested price |

Record confusion, avoidance, frustration, accessibility problems, and other unwanted effects. Agreement, session completion, and time spent in the tool do not by themselves show benefit.

## Business hypotheses
Possible customers to interview include educators, training organizations, and creative workshops. Their interest is not assumed. Possible revenue models are facilitator-led fictional-scenario workshops, software licensing, or paid setup and support. Price only after measuring costs and testing willingness to pay.

The commercial object is the tool, educational content, and support—not the sale of a person's disclosures. Keep access to personal records separate from purchasing decisions.

The ITI modular-kit concept could supply a portable demonstration station, but neither clinical use nor a new hardware purchase is required to test a paper or screen-based fictional exercise. Begin with the smallest useful version.

## Scope boundary
A proposed clinical application is a separate research track. This note does not describe a psychiatric intake protocol, stabilization system, approved therapy, or substitute for care. Moving into such use requires a defined intended purpose, qualified clinical leadership, appropriate review, and evidence. No vital-sign interpretation or patient experiment is offered here.
