# ITI Research Methodology

ITI distinguishes observations, hypotheses, proposals, decisions, and verified findings. Every research claim should identify its sources, assumptions, limitations, and validation status. Contradictory evidence and material corrections should be preserved, not quietly erased.

## Workflow
1. Capture the question and original context.
2. Classify its domain and maturity.
3. Investigate primary sources and competing explanations.
4. Outline testable claims.
5. Draft without treating conjecture as fact.
6. Critically review feasibility, cost, safety, and dependencies.
7. Verify with independent evidence or documented experiments.
8. Publish only a specifically approved version.
9. Revise transparently with a changelog.

## Status vocabulary
Idea, Researching, Outlined, Drafting, Review, Verified, Published, Archived. **Verified** must specify what was verified and by which method; it does not mean peer-reviewed. **Published** is a release state, not proof of correctness.

## Contributions
Researchers may suggest sources, objections, and independent approaches through a future published contact or issue channel. No contribution implies employment, compensation, endorsement, intellectual-property assignment, or permission to use ITI trademarks. A formal contribution and license policy must be approved before accepting external code or technical submissions.

## Privacy and integrity
Do not submit private personal information, credentials, confidential operational details, or potentially hazardous implementation instructions. AI assistance must not be represented as independent scientific validation.

## Context-preserving design capture
Keep original accounts, speaker corrections, observations, interpretations, design hypotheses, and evaluation results distinct. Mark self-report and transcript uncertainty. Preserve material disagreement; allow correction without erasing the source. Use synthetic examples for early prototypes and publish only the selected derivative.
