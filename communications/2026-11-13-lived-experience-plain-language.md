# Lived Experience Deserves a Place in Research

Draft for X Articles. Not posted. Author: Anthony P., founder of ITI. ChatGPT-assisted drafting. Based on the November 13, 2026 ITI publications notes.

I want to publish from where I stand: lived experience and practical questions from volunteering, EMS, surgical-laser support, and pest control. That background gives me questions to bring to the table. It does not make every answer I come up with correct.

The first step is being precise about what I am publishing. A personal observation is a personal observation. A product idea is a hypothesis. A study result needs an actual study. Publishing something in my repository does not make it peer-reviewed.

Here is the question behind the Foundry: could a person use a simple scene-building tool to explain an experience, correct misunderstandings, and separate what they know from what they assume?

We can test the earliest version with a fictional situation. Imagine someone planning a community event who pictures an empty venue. They conclude the project is finished before they have offered a booking.

Put that imagined scene on screen. Label what is known and what is feared. Ask what else could explain low attendance. Then choose one small test. The scene helps explain the concern. It does not prove the prediction.

A useful tool must let the person correct our interpretation. It should make AI suggestions visible, give people an easy way to stop, and keep personal information out unless it is actually needed.

The business possibility is a tool, a workshop, or support that people find useful enough to pay for. That possibility still needs testing. Any clinical application is a separate research question that needs qualified collaboration and evidence.

My aim is to keep the full reasoning in ITI's publications repository, then create clear slides and articles that point back to it. The plain-language version should keep the uncertainty and sources, not just the exciting part.

A lived-experience voice can contribute without pretending to be a physician. PCORI explicitly recognizes people with lived experience as research partners. That is a place at the table, with the same obligation to explain what our evidence can actually support.

Keep building. 🐜🕯️
