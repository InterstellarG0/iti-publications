# Everyday Lessons 04: Attention and Your Feed
**Organization:** Interstellar Technologies Initiative, LLC (ITI)  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Audience:** Ages 10–14  
**Status:** Prepared carousel text and narration; learning outcomes unevaluated

You can enjoy the scroll and still choose when to stop.

## Slide 01: Attention and your feed

You can enjoy the scroll and still choose when to stop.

**Notice. Choose. Switch.**

*Facilitator notes:* This lesson is about practicing choices, without guilt. Enjoying videos, games, or chatting is allowed.

## Slide 02: Your attention belongs to you

You can choose what gets your time.
You can change that choice.

**A pause is always an option.**

*Facilitator notes:* Connect to lessons one and two: a boundary can protect your time as well as your privacy.

## Slide 03: NOTICE

“I opened this for a game tip.
Am I still finding tips,
or just scrolling?”

**A quick check is enough.**

*Facilitator notes:* Model noticing without judging. No need to count every minute or keep checking repeatedly.

## Slide 04: How does this feel?

Interested? Having fun?
Tired? Annoyed? Comparing yourself?

**You can take a break at any point.**

*Facilitator notes:* Feelings are clues, not a test. You can enjoy something and still choose to stop.

## Slide 05: CHOOSE

“Do I want to keep watching,
find what I came for,
or do something else?”

**You get to choose your next move.**

*Facilitator notes:* Free time can include entertainment. The point is to make a choice rather than demand productivity every minute.

## Slide 06: SWITCH

Close the app.
Mute notifications for a while.
Put the device down.

**Try one move that works for you.**

*Facilitator notes:* Settings vary by app and device. Ask a trusted adult to help with unfamiliar settings. You do not have to change all settings at once.

## Slide 07: Friends can wait for a reply

“I’m logging off.
Catch you later.”

**Being a friend doesn’t mean always being online.**

*Facilitator notes:* Connect to friendship and peer pressure. You can say this calmly and leave. You do not have to stay to prove loyalty.

## Slide 08: Curiosity can become something

Found a drawing tip? Try a sketch.
Found a game trick? Practice it.

**Watching can be fun. Making can be fun, too.**

*Facilitator notes:* Invite a small experiment without requiring a finished product. Rest and entertainment also belong in a healthy day.

## Slide 09: Your turn

You came for a game tip.
Now you’re watching random clips.

**What would you notice, choose, and switch?**

*Facilitator notes:* See one: model, “I’m off track. I want to try that tip. I’ll close the feed and open the game.” Do one: let the learner choose their own response. Teach one: ask them to explain the steps to a friend using a fictional example.

## Slide 10: Notice. Choose. Switch.

You can change your mind.
You can start again.

**If stopping feels hard, ask someone you trust for help.**

*Facilitator notes:* End without shame. Practice takes repetition. A trusted adult can help make a plan, and asking for support is part of taking charge.
