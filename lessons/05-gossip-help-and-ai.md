# Everyday Lesson 05: Gossip, Help and AI
Audience: ages 10–14 with optional adult facilitation
Date: November 2026
Status: Educational draft; learning outcomes unevaluated
Origin: Founder-directed lesson with AI-assisted writing. All examples are fictional.
Theme: spring. This develops the existing planned Lesson 05 on checking claims and asking better questions.

## Original ten-slide copy
### 1. Gossip, help and AI
Talking about a problem can help.
What we say about another person still deserves care.

### 2. A message arrives
“Everyone says Sam cheated.”
You did not see it happen.
What do you actually know?

### 3. What you saw
“I saw Sam look toward another desk.”
That describes an action.
It does not tell you why.

### 4. The missing pieces
You can say:
“I don't know what happened yet.”
Repeating a rumor does not check it.

### 5. Asking for help
“I felt embarrassed in our chat.
Can you help me choose a response?”
You can ask for support without insulting someone.

### 6. Sharing less
Use made-up names in practice.
Leave out addresses and private screenshots.
Ask a trusted adult before using an unfamiliar AI app.

### 7. AI can guess wrong
An AI reply may sound sure and still be mistaken.
Ask: “What are you assuming?
What else could explain it?”

### 8. A kind correction
“Thanks for helping.
That detail is wrong.
Please check it again.”
You can be kind and still check the answer.

### 9. A response you choose
“I want to talk without insults.”
You can also leave, mute or ask for help.
Threats or repeated harm deserve adult support right away.

### 10. Your practice
A friend says, “Everyone hates me.”
What do you know?
What could you ask?
Who could help?

## Facilitator notes
Distinguish an observation, interpretation and rumor without asking learners to reveal private experiences. Gossip can mean talking about an absent person; the lesson focuses on accuracy, care and appropriate help rather than labeling every disclosure as bad.
A learner can pass. Private help-seeking about bullying or harm is appropriate. Never require direct confrontation, screenshots or proof before offering support. Follow the actual app's age requirements. For younger learners, an adult can read a fictional AI reply aloud without creating an account.
The final scenario invites questions such as “What happened?” and “Would you like help talking to an adult?” Avoid pretending to know others' thoughts.
AI agreement does not establish truth. App privacy settings and retention differ. Avoid promising confidential conversations.
Evaluate understanding with a new fictional example, not likes, shares or time spent online.

## Practice prompt supplement (November 2026)

The revised carousel adds an eleventh practice slide. The original ten teaching slides retain their content.

```text
In a fictional class, I saw Sam look toward another desk. Someone says Sam cheated. Separate what I saw from guesses. Give two possible explanations and one fair question. Do not decide that Sam is guilty.
```

Check the answer against the supplied facts and limits. Practice on paper with a facilitator if an AI service is unsuitable. Use fictional details and follow service age requirements.

Small footer on each revised slide: *AI can be wrong. Check important answers. Keep personal information private.*
